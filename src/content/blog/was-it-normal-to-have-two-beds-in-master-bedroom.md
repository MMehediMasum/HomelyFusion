## Historical Bedroom Setups

Bedrooms have changed a lot through history. The idea of having two beds in a master bedroom is not new. It reflects different customs and needs of the past.

Looking at old bedroom setups helps us understand why some furniture choices were common. This includes why people sometimes had two beds instead of one.

### Sleeping Customs Through The Ages

In some times, people shared beds for warmth or comfort. Other times, two beds were used to give each person more space. Beds were also arranged based on social status or family size.

1. Ancient Rome: Families often slept in the same room but used separate beds.
2. Medieval Europe: Two beds in a room could mean different sleeping times or privacy.
3. Victorian Era: Separate beds were common for married couples to keep cleanliness.
4. Early 20th Century: Some homes had two beds for children or guests in the master room.

* Partners with different mattress preferences get better support.
* Helps manage conditions like sleep apnea or restless leg syndrome.

The size of the master bedroom is key. Two beds need enough floor space for movement. The room layout also matters to place beds without crowding.

* Measure the room before choosing beds.
* Leave at least two feet of space between beds and walls.
* Plan space for bedside tables and walking paths.
* Consider window and door placement.
